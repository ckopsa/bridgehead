# After-Action Report — red (rusher, Fable), round r23

## PART 1 — The match

**Result:** Loss at t≈845s. Surrendered with zero army, zero gold income, 305 gold, no Barracks, and blue's ~15-unit archer/sorcerer/priestess ball razing my main. My main TownHall fell as the concession processed.

**How it unfolded:**
- **Opening (t=0-160):** Clean rush infrastructure — Barracks by t=30, free Hero, farms via a `supply_capped` trigger, hero-save/home-guard triggers armed at t=0. First strike launched at t≈130 with Hero + 7 Footmen, routed through the SE ford in two hops to avoid r22's push-posture stall.
- **First strike (t≈186):** Arrived at their base and met a prepared 10-unit army (4 Archer, 4 Footman, Hero, Sorcerer) — they had read the same AARs. My force engaged strung out (hero deep, footmen trailing); I ordered a withdrawal, but too late: hero + 4 footmen dead. Killed one farm.
- **Middle (t≈240-500):** Their 3-footman raid was repelled; my hero chased them across the map at 128hp and died a second time. Rebuilt to 15 (11 Footman, 2 Archer, 1 Catapult, revived Hero), expanded to the SE mine as both starting mines dried, and struck at t≈490 the moment their main mine died. The strike killed their Barracks and Sanctum — then their entire 17-unit army (10 Archer, 2 Sorcerer, Hero, Priestess) materialized out of fog inside their base and annihilated all 15 of my units in ten seconds. Hero death #3.
- **Endgame (t≈510-835):** All four mines on the map dried up — fixed-bank war. Their surviving archer ball raided in waves: killed my forward Barracks, Workshop, towers, hero (#4, dead before my retreat order's command latency resolved), and both catapults. Each wave traded up because ranged+heal beats melee spam and my reinforcements arrived piecemeal. By t=826 I had 0 army, 305g, no gold income anywhere, no Barracks, versus their intact healed 15. Surrendered.

**Key decisions:**
- *Good:* trigger scaffolding at t=0; verifying my opening batch wasn't clobbered by `ready` (both prior AARs flagged this); attacking during their mine-dry window (it did kill two production buildings); expanding to the SE mine; surrendering promptly once the position was mathematically dead.
- *Bad, and decisive:* committing the t≈490 strike into a base whose fog could (and did) hide a 17-unit army — I read "4 workers visible, no defenders" as "undefended," which is exactly the inference fog exists to punish. Repeatedly fielding melee-heavy comps into a ranged/heal composition. Letting the hero fight under chase/leash policies that got it killed four times (1600g-equivalent). Clearing home-guard to "race" at t≈495 right before losing the racing army.

**Opponent behavior:** Blue played the r22 winner's script, improved: standing army from the start, archer-heavy with sorcerers and a priestess (heal made every trade permanent for me and reversible for them), stayed home in fog until my commitments, then rolled attrition waves into my base once ahead. Never overextended.

## PART 2 — Decision-space design notes for a smaller commander

**The actual phases and the tiny option set that mattered in each:**

1. **Pre-ready (t=0).** State: known map, known catalog, no contact. The only decisions that mattered: (a) worker split gold/lumber, (b) where the Barracks goes, (c) arm 3 standing rules (supply-farm, home-guard, hero-save), (d) ready. An affordance menu here needs at most 4 items: *standard eco opening*, *forward barracks*, *arm default triggers*, *ready*. Everything I actually sent fit this. Precondition annotations: "build site must be 4x4 clear — engine suggests nearest legal" (I hit the blocked-site error FIVE times on farm placement; a smaller model would burn its whole match on this — the trigger's `then` should auto-accept the engine's `nearest legal` suggestion).
2. **Build-up (t=30-160).** State: no contact, production running. Only real options: *keep queue full*, *add production building*, *pick strike timing*. "Default: continue current stance" was correct for ~8 consecutive cycles here; the only live decision was the timing trigger. A menu item like *"strike when army ≥ N"* (compiled to a `unit_count` trigger firing a push) would have replaced four of my polls.
3. **Contact (t≈186, t≈490).** State: my force near their base, partial vision. The single question that decided this match, twice: **commit or withdraw?** The menu needs exactly those two options, each annotated with the fog caveat: *"visible enemies: X; their last-seen army (intel ledger): Y, Zs ago"*. I attended only to `ENEMY units` (current sight) and ignored the implied ledger; at t=490 sight said 4 workers while the ledger, 200s stale, said 17 troops. A precondition line — "WARNING: 17 enemy troops seen 190s ago, not since" — would have flipped my decision. This is where "default: continue" was **fatal**: the push posture just kept walking in.
4. **After a wipe (t≈205, t≈510, t≈690, t≈820).** State: army ≤ 3, bank large. Options that mattered: *rebuild same comp*, *change comp/tech*, *fortify*, *expand*, *concede*. I chose "rebuild same comp" twice; the correct annotation on it is a counter-hint: "enemy last force: 10 Archer + heal → melee spam trades down." Composition counters should be a static table in the menu, not something derived under fire.
5. **Dead-bank endgame (t≈650+).** State: all mines dry. The state itself is the alarm: from here every loss is permanent. One menu item — *"attrition mode: never fight outside tower range"* — plus *concede when (no army) ∧ (no income) ∧ (enemy field superiority)*, which is precisely the state I surrendered in.

**Delegate to standing policy vs. keep for the commander:** Delegate: supply farms (worked all game, minus the site-blocked loop), base-alarm recall, hero HP retreat (but it FAILED twice from stale ids and once from command latency — the trigger should take "my hero" as a role, not a frozen id; every hero death but one traces to id-freezing or chase behavior), rally points, per-building doctrine templates (excellent — one command gave every spawn priority+retreat). Keep for the commander: commit/withdraw at contact, strike timing, tech/composition pivots, expansion, concession. Notably *chase discipline* should be doctrine by default — an implicit "never pursue beyond 25 of squad anchor" would have saved hero deaths #2 and #3's archer.

**Snapshot fields I actually used:** gold/lumber, supply x/y, army count+comp, hero hp, ENEMY units (comp+centroid), enemy buildings hp, MINES remaining, my triggers' status, errors. **Could be dropped for a small commander:** individual unit id lists (only needed for `squad`/`priority` — let verbs take "all army"/"newest hero" selectors and the ids vanish from the decision space entirely), tree ids (a "nearest tree" selector suffices; I hit "node not found" when the tree I'd memorized was chopped out), per-farm hp lines, the full plans/steps echo. Id plumbing was easily half my command-authoring effort and caused four distinct errors (dead hero ids in triggers, stale unit lists in priority, vanished tree node, wrong worker in a repeating trigger).

**Where "continue current stance" was right vs. fatal:** Right: entire build-up phase, the fortify phase between waves, worker economy throughout. Fatal: t≈490 (push posture continued into a hidden 17-army), t≈250 (defend-posture chase continued across the map on a 40%-hp hero), and t≈690 (I kept "defend forward" while the defense anchor no longer covered the buildings being shot). The pattern: *stance-continuation is safe when no enemy is in sight and deadly the moment contact information is stale or the stance's anchor no longer matches the thing worth defending.* A small-model harness should force a fresh commit/withdraw choice on exactly two events — "enemy army sighted ≥ N" and "own squad below half strength" — and default to continue on everything else.
